Know the laws • Know your space • Read instructions

Celebrate safely.

Consumer fireworks are powerful pyrotechnic devices. This practical guide is adapted from Natural Resources Canada’s official guidance for buying, storing, and using consumer fireworks.

✓Adults only: You must be at least 18 to buy, store, and use consumer fireworks. An adult must supervise anyone under 18.
Before you light

Plan the show before the show.

Check municipal restrictions and fire bans first. Read every product label, understand the required spectator distance, inspect the launch area, and make sure the wind carries sparks away from people and property.

1

Know the laws

Federal rules apply across Canada, and provinces and municipalities may add restrictions on when and where fireworks can be used.

2

Choose a clear outdoor space

Stay away from buildings, vehicles, overhead obstructions, dry brush, and other flammable materials. Follow label distances.

3

Read every instruction

Plan the firing order, understand each effect, and follow the manufacturer’s setup instructions exactly.

4

Watch the weather

Never fire in windy conditions. Do not proceed when grass or brush is dry or when a fire ban is active.

5

Secure the firing base

Set fireworks on firm ground. Use earth or sand as directed, and angle unsupported items about 10 degrees away from people.

Prepare and protect

Keep these essentials close.

Water ready

Keep a charged hose or bucket of water beside the firing area.

Eyes protected

Safety glasses are recommended for the person lighting the display.

Hands clear

Never hold or pick up a lit firework unless the label specifically says it is hand-held.

Fuse at the tip

Light carefully at the tip of the fuse, then move back to the required distance.

Children supervised

Sparklers burn extremely hot and can ignite clothing or cause injury.

Storage secure

Keep fireworks cool, dry, ventilated, away from ignition sources, and inaccessible to children.

After firing

Misfires need patience—not a second match.

Wait at least 30 minutes

Do not approach a firework that failed to ignite until at least 30 minutes have passed. Never attempt to relight or repair it.

Soak and dispose

Soak used fireworks and debris in a bucket of water. Confirm there are no smouldering remnants before household disposal.
